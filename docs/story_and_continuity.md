# Story and continuity guide

This document is the narrative reference for translation, runtime QA, and scene-order
review. The Japanese game script is authoritative. The OVA is a continuity/voice
reference only.

## Core continuity

The game is serialized more strongly than the mission titles imply.

- **Mission 0** is Sengoku's pre-Cyber-Police origin story.
- **Mission 1** establishes the Westanica/Isteria intelligence conflict and the
  new Sengoku/Varsus/Okyo working arrangement.
- **Missions 2-8** form one continuous conspiracy centered on Dr. Raizo Fuwa's
  human genetic research, the DNA catalyst EDX, and foreign attempts to obtain or
  suppress that technology.

The main retrospective chain is:

```text
Fuwa's illegal genetic research
        |
        v
Fuwa experiments on Kunihiko Takiguchi
        |
        v
Takiguchi seeks revenge through Isterian Intelligence
        |
        v
Takiguchi manipulates Kyoichi Isaka
        |
        v
Isaka steals EDX
        |
        v
Fuwa's beast-man research advances
        |
        v
Takiguchi transforms and kills Isaka
        |
        v
"serial murder" investigation
        |
        v
Fuwa / foreign-intelligence connection exposed
        |
        v
Redhead and Blue Eyes identified as Westanican agents
        |
        v
Takiguchi confesses; Isterian hideout located
        |
        v
Okyo kidnapped
        |
        v
final assault and collapse of the project
```

## Identity and relationship map

| Presented identity | True identity / role | Reveal boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Asuka | **Tomomi Fuwa**, Dr. Fuwa's eldest daughter | Final mission |
| Blue Eyes | Asuka / Tomomi; Westanican Intelligence | Missions 5-6 identity layer |
| Redhead | **Rudolf**, Westanican Intelligence | Missions 5-6 identity layer |
| Kunihiko | **Kunihiko Takiguchi**, Fuwa test subject / beast-man | Missions 6-7 |
| Dr. Fuwa | **Raizo Fuwa**, geneticist and origin of the beast-man project | Missions 4-8 |

Translation must preserve the player's discovery order. Do not use a later identity
in earlier dialogue merely because the final localization knows it.

## Fuwa family

- **Raizo Fuwa** — genetic researcher; expelled from legitimate research after
  crossing ethical boundaries.
- **Yukie Fuwa** — wife; killed after Fuwa uses her as an experimental subject.
- **Tomomi Fuwa** — eldest daughter; officially believed dead, actually survives
  and becomes the Westanican intelligence operative known as Asuka / Blue Eyes.
- **Saori Fuwa** — younger daughter; another victim of Fuwa's research. At the end
  she survives but requires cold sleep to suppress the altered beast cells.

Tomomi knows the truth about her mother. Saori was too young to understand it.

## Mission chronology

### Mission 0 — Origin

Sengoku returns to Oedo after seven years while pursuing cybercriminal Chris
Danford ("Rose Rash"). Old City has changed: the Security Force has expanded,
weapons are everywhere, and Sengoku learns that Benten and Gogul are also in
Oedo.

The Security Force / Makze crisis draws Sengoku into Maya's investigation.
Maya is shot, Sengoku is arrested after the headquarters confrontation, and
Hasegawa offers him the choice between his enormous sentence and service as a
mobile detective.

**Continuity rule:** Sengoku, Benten, and Gogul are rivals / uneasy associates
here, not the settled team they become later.

### Mission 1 — Ambassador Escort

Sengoku's first assignment as a mobile detective is protection of a Westanican
ambassador. Varsus and Okyo become part of his operating team.

The mission establishes the diplomatic conflict between Westanica and Isteria
and introduces two people who will matter later: Rudolf and Asuka. Their true
intelligence roles are not yet public knowledge.

### Mission 2 — Classified File

Dr. Chang's institute develops **EDX**, a DNA catalyst that dramatically
improves genetic manipulation. Classified manufacturing information is stolen.

Kyoichi Isaka becomes the main suspect. He has serious gambling debt, giving him
an apparent financial motive. The trail includes the false identity used by the
man later known as Redhead and technical evidence pointing to Isaka's access.

At this stage the player is meant to believe Isaka may be the principal thief,
not that he is a manipulated middleman.

### Mission 3 — The Suspect

The investigation follows Isaka through his Old City contacts, including Lisa
and his university connections. Kunihiko Takiguchi enters the evidentiary chain
through his connection to Fuwa's institute.

Isaka is then murdered with impossible physical force. Benten pursues the
killer; Sengoku later finds Benten's wrecked bike, blood, and weapon but no body.
Benten is treated as presumed dead.

**Do not translate early Mission 3-4 dialogue as though anyone knows Benten
survived.**

### Mission 4 — Serial Murders

More victims appear with injuries consistent with superhuman strength. Forensic
work finds evidence of DNA alteration.

The sample technology resembles instantaneous physical enhancement, but the
particular evidence does not show that EDX itself was used. The investigation
points toward Dr. Raizo Fuwa, whose work was already in this field.

The case changes from "who stole the file?" into "what human experimentation
program produced this killer?"

### Mission 5 — Dr. Fuwa Missing

Hasegawa explicitly orders the team to connect **EDX, Fuwa's disappearance, and
Isaka's death**.

Fuwa's family history is uncovered. His wife died in connection with his
research; Tomomi was later officially reported dead; Saori disappeared with
Fuwa.

Isaka's notes show that he was compromised and manipulated rather than the
architect of the conspiracy. Takiguchi's relationship to Fuwa and Isterian
activity becomes increasingly important.

The Westanican side is also revealed to have a direct interest in Fuwa. Redhead
and Blue Eyes emerge as operational identities.

### Mission 6 — Red Hair and Blue Eyes

The Westanican ambassador is murdered by the same kind of overwhelming physical
force associated with the serial killings, even though the earlier presumed
killer is already gone. The implication is that more than one transformed human
exists.

Westanican intelligence officers formally join the investigation:

- Redhead = Rudolf
- Blue Eyes = Asuka

Gogul tails Takiguchi. Takiguchi is connected to Asuka personally as well as to
Fuwa's research. The team investigates Fuwa's transformation drug.

Takiguchi ultimately transforms into a beast-man. Rudolf dies protecting
Tomomi/Asuka, Gogul is badly injured, and Takiguchi is left dying but conscious
long enough for the next mission's confession.

### Mission 7 — Kidnapping

Takiguchi gives the central confession.

He hated Fuwa for turning him into a monster. He used Isterian Intelligence as
a weapon against Fuwa, manipulated his old university friend Isaka, and had
Isaka steal EDX for the research.

Takiguchi also admits that he killed Isaka while transformed and barely aware of
his actions. Once the transformation starts, he loses rational control and sees
everyone around him as an enemy.

He says Fuwa completed a "perfect" beast-man and gives Tomomi the location of
the Isterian hideout, asking her to destroy Fuwa's research.

Isterian operatives then begin eliminating witnesses and associates. Dr. Chang
is killed. Okyo is kidnapped, turning the conspiracy into a direct hostage
crisis.

### Mission 8 — The Hideout

Hasegawa orders Sengoku to stop. Sengoku refuses and moves against the Isterian
hideout despite the institutional and personal risk.

Varsus's legal/programmed obligations temporarily put him in conflict with
Sengoku. During the assault, Benten returns alive and Gogul re-enters the fight.
The prior presumed death of Benten is therefore intentional uncertainty, not a
continuity error.

Asuka finally reveals her true identity:

**Tomomi Fuwa, Dr. Fuwa's eldest daughter.**

Tomomi explains that Fuwa experimented on and killed her mother, later used
Takiguchi as a human experiment, and lost essentially all ethical restraint.
Takiguchi attempted to use Isterian Intelligence to take revenge. The incidents
from the EDX theft onward all converge on this struggle over Fuwa's technology.

Saori survives the project but her altered cells cannot simply be cured. Cold
sleep is used to suppress their activity.

The public account is sanitized into a terrorism/rescue incident rather than an
admission that foreign intelligence services fought over a human genetic-weapons
program in Oedo.

## Character knowledge gates

Use these as translation QA rules.

1. **Mission 0:** Benten/Gogul are not yet settled coworkers.
2. **Mission 2:** Isaka is still a plausible primary culprit.
3. **Mission 3:** Takiguchi is not yet publicly identified as a beast-man.
4. **Missions 3-4:** Benten is presumed dead; no one should speak with knowledge
   of his later survival.
5. **Before Mission 6 briefing:** Redhead/Blue Eyes must not be casually labeled
   as Westanican intelligence unless the specific speaker already knows.
6. **Before Mission 7 confession:** Isaka's theft must not be described as a
   fully understood Takiguchi operation.
7. **Before Mission 8 reveal:** Asuka must not be called Tomomi Fuwa by characters
   who have not independently discovered the identity.
8. **Takiguchi:** preserve both agency and victimhood. He commits serious crimes
   and manipulates Isaka, but Fuwa also transformed him into an unstable human
   experiment.
9. **Isaka:** do not retroactively write him as mastermind. His financial
   desperation and manipulation are essential.
10. **Fuwa:** the final narrative cause is his research and abuse, even though
    Isteria and Westanica exploit the situation for state interests.

## Translation implications

- Preserve aliases until their reveal points.
- Preserve uncertainty ("missing", "presumed dead", "suspect") instead of
  translating from omniscient knowledge.
- Keep institutional terminology distinct: Cyber Police, Security Force,
  Westanican Intelligence, Isterian Intelligence.
- Treat Missions 2-8 as one accumulating investigation when choosing callbacks
  and terminology.
- Do not flatten Takiguchi, Isaka, Rudolf, or Tomomi into simple
  villain/ally labels; later revelations deliberately reinterpret earlier
  scenes.

## QA use

During playtest, log for every mandatory scene:

- mission and resource;
- speaker;
- player-visible clue;
- identities known to that speaker;
- whether the line is mandatory or optional;
- whether it can be seen before another clue;
- screenshot/save-state evidence.

That log is the final authority for ambiguous reveal ordering when static bank
order and runtime scene order disagree.
